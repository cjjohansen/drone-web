Who tells the storefront about a product?

Historical record. This page compared the options before the published contracts were chosen. Option D was chosen (D-037 in .ralph/agent/decisions.md) and now lives in design/contracts/, where the channels were renamed (for example product-specifications, effective-prices, product-availabilities). The channel names below are the ones the research used.

The storefront was designed to read full product snapshots. The write side (catalog-management-v2) sends only IDs, and nothing sends a price or spec change. Before anything on the read side can be built, we need one contract. Below are three options, each as AsyncAPI 3.0, plus OpenAPI 3.1 where a call is synchronous.

The clash

Storefront expects

design/catalog-storefront/addr/refine/integration-events.yaml

  • catalog.product.created, .updated: the full product state (name, brand, sku, status, specifications, categories, pricing, availability)
  • catalog.product.price-changed, .availability-changed, .discontinued
  • Consumers: Compatibility API, Partner Catalog
Write side sends

design/catalog-management-v2/addr/refine/integration-events.yaml

  • product.created {productId}
  • product.lifecycle-state-changed {productId, lifecycleState}
  • price.group-repricing-requested {repricingJobId}
  • inventory.quantity-updated {inventoryId, productId}

What's missing

  • No event for a spec change or a single price change, although the APIs have updateProductSpecifications and setBasePrice.
  • boundary-map.md lists ProductSpecificationUpdated and ProductActivated, but the AsyncAPI file doesn't have them.
  • The envelopes differ: timestamp vs occurredAt. Event naming (OQ-007) is still open.
  • Two places claim to own product facts: the storefront's Catalog API ("owns product data") and Product Lifecycle (the strategy's P2: "the only writer of product facts").

What the merged strategy says: P2 says one writer per fact. P3 says cross-boundary events carry the full state, so consumers don't call back (LRN-006), and there are no synchronous calls between boundaries while handling a request. #14 adds: "something assembles the snapshot, and that something needs an owner."

Data mesh lens

Who produces product data, who joins it, and who reads what

Dehghani (Data Mesh, 2022, ch. 4 "Domain Data Archetypes") describes three kinds of data product: source-aligned (facts from the system that produces them), aggregate (combines several upstream domains) and consumer-aligned (reshaped for a specific use). Bellemare (2023, ch. 2) applies them to event streams and advises using consumer-aligned products sparingly.

The wording is paraphrased; check it against the book. Watch out: Bellemare uses "aggregate" for sums (hourly sales per store), while Dehghani uses it for a join across domains. This page uses Dehghani's sense.

Options through this lens

A Product Lifecycle republishes price and stock. That breaks ownership: Pricing and Inventory stop being products anyone can consume, and lineage collapses into one productVersion.

B Source-aligned streams carry IDs only. State is pulled, every consumer does its own join, and P3's "no synchronous calls" is broken.

C Owner fact events plus one shared Product View with sourceVersions. Closest to a mesh, but it assumes every consumer wants the same join.

D One source-aligned fact stream per owner. Each consumer owns its aggregate (Catalog the full view, Partner its feed). Compatibility reads specs only. Lineage goes in the envelope (source, sourceVersion, origin from Ingestion, derivedFrom) and in EventCatalog. A shared assembler only appears once two consumers want the same join.

The contract drafts for A, B and C

Event-carried state transfer

Product Lifecycle publishes the whole product as a snapshot every time any part of it changes. To include price and stock, it listens to Pricing and Inventory and publishes again. Consumers simply replace their copy with the newest productVersion.

flowchart LR
  PR[Pricing] -- price events --> PL[Product Lifecycle]
  IN[Inventory] -- stock events --> PL
  PL -- "catalog.product.published / updated
(full snapshot)" --> K[(compacted topic)]
  K --> CAT[Catalog API]
  K --> CMP[Compatibility API]
  K --> PAR[Partner Catalog]

For

  • Matches P3 and LRN-006 as they're written
  • The storefront contract barely changes
  • One producer and one version, so ordering is simple
  • Least work on the read side

Against

  • Product Lifecycle republishes prices and stock it doesn't own, which blurs P2
  • Ties the Product Data team to the Commercial team's events
  • One wide schema that every change touches
  • A repricing job causes a storm of snapshots

Notification + callback

Each owner announces only "product X changed, now at version N". The storefront's projectors then fetch the current state over HTTP, outside any buyer request. The fetch is a synchronous contract, so it has its own OpenAPI file.

flowchart LR
  PL[Product Lifecycle] -- product.changed --> P[Storefront projectors]
  PR[Pricing] -- price.changed --> P
  IN[Inventory] -- availability.changed --> P
  P -. "GET /products/{id}
If-None-Match" .-> PL
  P -. GET /price-book-entries/{id} .-> PR
  P -. GET /availability/{id} .-> IN

For

  • Each owner talks only about its own facts
  • Tiny events; the latest state always comes from its owner
  • The version and ETag let consumers skip fetches they don't need

Against

  • Goes against P3 and LRN-006 ("without calling back"), so it needs a recorded exception
  • The read side depends on the write side being up
  • Three new GET endpoints (v2 has none)
  • Every projector puts the product together on its own, three times over

Domain events + Product View

Each owner publishes small events that carry the changed values. A Product View assembler keeps one row per product and publishes a composed snapshot. All read sides subscribe to that one topic. The assembler makes no calls, so this option needs no OpenAPI file.

flowchart LR
  PL[Product Lifecycle] -- "product.specification-updated
product.lifecycle-state-changed" --> PV[Product View assembler]
  PR[Pricing] -- price.base-price-set --> PV
  IN[Inventory] -- inventory.availability-changed --> PV
  PV -- "storefront.product-view.updated
(snapshot + sourceVersions)" --> K[(compacted topic)]
  K --> CAT[Catalog API]
  K --> CMP[Compatibility API]
  K --> PAR[Partner Catalog]

For

  • Clearest ownership: one producer per fact (P2, #14's teams)
  • Read sides keep one snapshot shaped like today's ProductEvent
  • Adds the missing spec and price events that both PRs ask for
  • No callbacks, so P3 holds

Against

  • One more component, and it needs an owner
  • Needs a version per source to keep things in order
  • Must buffer partial views (a price arriving before its product)
  • Most work up front; the view is eventually consistent

  


  

    
Recommendation at the time (revised after the data mesh view)

    
D: one fact stream per owner; each consumer owns its join

    
Seen as a data mesh, the owners (Product Lifecycle, Pricing, Inventory, Category, Documentation) are source-aligned data products. Each publishes facts with values, not only IDs. That is the event set from C. Catalog and Partner Catalog are consumer-aligned and each builds its own view. Compatibility only reads specs. A shared Product View (C's assembler) appears only once two consumers want exactly the same join. Lineage goes in every envelope (source, sourceVersion, origin from Ingestion, derivedFrom) and is shown in EventCatalog. Ingestion must start publishing product.imported, so we can tell where a product version came from. Every persona rates that as critical. D was chosen; its published contracts are in design/contracts/.

  


  

    
Questions that were open

    
    
      
  1. Is Product Lifecycle the source of product facts, or a curated copy of the ERP and supplier data? If it's a copy, must each field say where it came from? The storefront storm leaves this open (event-storming.md:341).
    Answer: a curated copy of the ERP and supplier files; each published fact carries origin (D-037, rule 5 in design/contracts/README.md).
    2. 
      
  2. Which line of P2 holds? strategy.md:68 says "only writer of product facts", but line 69 says "one producer per fact".
    Answer: one producer per fact; each owner publishes its own stream (D-037).
    3. 
      
  3. Should each consumer own its own join (D), or should one shared Product View serve them all (C)? If shared, which team owns it?
    Answer: each consumer owns its join; a join becomes a shared Product View with a named owner once two consumers need the same rule (rules 3 and 4 in design/contracts/README.md).
    4. 
      
  4. Lineage in every event, in EventCatalog, or both?
    Answer: both: provenance in the CloudEvents envelope (shared/envelope.yaml), product-to-product lineage in EventCatalog (rules 6 and 7).
    5. 
      
  5. Are Flight Data Hub and Channel Syndication Platform upstream sources in scope, or only background?
    Answer: background only; the ERP is upstream and sits behind one anticorruption layer (D-037).

  6. Close OQ-007 (event naming) at the same time?
    Answer: not closed with D-037; OQ-007 is still listed as open. The published contracts follow the naming conventions in design/contracts/README.md.
